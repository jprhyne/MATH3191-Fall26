    \question[10] Determine if the following $3$ vectors are linearly independent.
    \[
        \begin{bmatrix}
            1\\0\\3
        \end{bmatrix} \qquad
        \begin{bmatrix}
            3\\2\\-1
        \end{bmatrix}\qquad
        \begin{bmatrix}
            1\\1\\-1
        \end{bmatrix}
    \]
    \begin{solution}
        There are many ways to solve this one. Since we are going to invert a matrix whose columns
        are the above vectors, we will go ahead and try to compute the inverse here to save computation

        \begin{eqnarray}
            \aMat{c|c}{
                A & I
            } &=& \aMat{ccc|ccc}{
                1 & 3 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
                0 & 2 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\
                3 & -1 & -1 & 0 & 0 & 1
            }\nonumber\\
            &\xrightarrow{R_3\gets R_3 - 3R_1}&\aMat{ccc|ccc}{
                1 & 3 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
                0 & 2 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\
                0 & -10 & -4 & -3 & 0 & 1
            }\nonumber\\
            &\xrightarrow{R_3\gets R_3 + 5R_2}&\aMat{ccc|ccc}{
                1 & 3 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
                0 & 2 & 1 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\
                0 & 0 & 1 & -3 & 5 & 1
            }\label{eq:canStop}\\
            &\xrightarrow{R_2\gets\frac{1}{2}R_2}&\aMat{ccc|ccc}{
                1 & 3 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
                0 & 1 & \frac{1}{2} & 0 & \frac{1}{2} & 0 \\
                0 & 0 & 1 & -3 & 5 & 1
            }\nonumber\\
            &\xrightarrow{R_2\gets R_2 - \frac{1}{2}R_3}&\aMat{ccc|ccc}{
                1 & 3 & 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
                0 & 1 & 0 & \frac{3}{2} & -2 & -\frac{1}{2} \\
                0 & 0 & 1 & -3 & 5 & 1
            }\nonumber\\
            &\xrightarrow{R_1\gets R_1 - R_3}&\aMat{ccc|ccc}{
                1 & 3 & 0 & 4 & -5 & -1 \\
                0 & 1 & 0 & \frac{3}{2} & -2 & -\frac{1}{2} \\
                0 & 0 & 1 & -3 & 5 & 1
            }\nonumber\\
            &\xrightarrow{R_1\gets R_1 - 3R_2}&\aMat{ccc|ccc}{
                1 & 0 & 0 & -\frac{1}{2} & 1 & \frac{1}{2} \\
                0 & 1 & 0 & \frac{3}{2} & -2 & -\frac{1}{2} \\
                0 & 0 & 1 & -3 & 5 & 1
            }\nonumber
        \end{eqnarray}

        Since we were able to compute the inverse, the columns of the original matrix we computed
        were linearly independent as desired.

        Note that we could have stopped at \ref{eq:canStop} if we were only interested in if
        these three vectors were linearly independent. Since we have 3 pivot columns, we know these
        three are linearly independent. However, as we stated above, we will want the inverse
        for a later problem, so we keep going!
    \end{solution}

    \question[10]Let $A\in\R^{3\times 3}$ such that
    \[
        A = \begin{bmatrix}
            1 & 3 & 1 \\
            0 & 2 & 1 \\
            3 & -1 & -1
        \end{bmatrix}
    \]
    Solve the following system for $\vec{x}$ by using $A^{-1}$

    \[
        \bMat{
            1 & 3 & 1 \\
            0 & 2 & 1 \\
            3 & -1 & -1}\bmat{x_1\\x_2\\x_3} = \bMat{7 \\ 9 \\-11 }
    \]
    \begin{solution}
        Since we computed the inverse of the above matrix in problem 1, we just have to multiply
        both sides by $A^{-1}$.

        \begin{align*}
            \bMat{x_1\\x_2\\x_3}    &= \bMat{-\frac{1}{2} & 1 & \frac{1}{2}\\
                                         \frac{3}{2} & -2 & -\frac{1}{2}\\
                                         -3 & 5 & 1}\bMat{7\\9\\-11}\\
                &= 7\bMat{ -\frac{1}{2} \\ \frac{3}{2} \\ -3 }
                + 9\bMat{ 1\\-2\\5 } - 11\bMat{\frac{1}{2} \\ -\frac{1}{2} \\ 1} \\
                &= \bMat{-\frac{7}{2} + 9 - \frac{11}{2}\\
                         \frac{21}{2} - 18 + \frac{11}{2}\\
                         -21 + 45 - 11} \\
                &= \bMat{
                    0 \\
                    -2 \\
                    13
                }
        \end{align*}
    \end{solution}
